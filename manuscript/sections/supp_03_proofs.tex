\section{Learning bounds and coordinate inference proofs}\label{supp:proofs}

\subsection{Uniform nonsmooth profile gradients}

Select the minimum Euclidean norm dual optimiser at bandwidth zero
and denote its induced coefficient subgradient by $g_{i,0}^{c}(\beta)$.
For a fixed $\beta$ it is the almost surely unique dual score of
Proposition~\ref{prop:supp-dual-profile}. Its expectation is
$\nabla R_0(\beta)$ under the density and moment conditions.

\begin{lemma}[Sparse gradient fluctuation]\label{lem:supp-gradient}
For the sparse coefficient ball and unit directions whose combined
support has size at most a fixed multiple of $D$, let $L_n=\log(2np)$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-gradient-rate}
 \sup_{\beta,v}|(P_T-P)v^Tg_{i,0}^{c}(\beta)|
   =O_p\!\left\{\sqrt{D L_n/n}+(D L_n)^{3/2}/n\right\}
   =O_p(\delta_n).
\end{equation}
If $u$ supplies an $\epsilon_n$-subgradient of the empirical profile
risk at a sparse coefficient, its directional difference from the
population gradient is $O_p(\delta_n+\sqrt{\epsilon_n})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The dual box has dimension at most $m_{\max}$. On each lower, upper
and free-coordinate pattern, its KKT equations are linear in the
free dual coordinates and in $Y-X\beta$. Minimum norm selection on a
possibly singular face is a parametric quadratic problem with a
fixed finite number of constraints. Enumerating its active sets gives
affine solution pieces. Feasibility and comparisons between pieces
are polynomial inequalities of bounded degree, with their number
depending on the bounded cluster size. A projected score subgraph
therefore has polynomial sign complexity in $O(D)$ real parameters
on a given support. Warren's sign count, including the equality
extension in \citeA{goldberg1995}, gives covering logarithm
$CD\log(Cp/\varepsilon)$ after taking the union over supports.

Every projected score is bounded in absolute value by
$\max_j|X_j^Tv|$. Its second moment is uniformly bounded. The observed
envelope over the sparse unit directions is $O_p(\sqrt{D L_n})$.
Symmetrisation and the entropy bound give a variance contribution
$C\sqrt{D L_n/n}$. Bernstein truncation gives an envelope
contribution $C\sqrt{D L_n}(D L_n)/n$. Sub-Gaussian tails make the
truncation remainder smaller than these terms. This proves
\eqref{eq:supp-gradient-rate}.

Let $f_T=P_T\ell_{i,0}^{\mathrm{prof}}$. For an epsilon subgradient
$u$ and a unit sparse direction $v$,
\[
 \frac{f_T(\beta)-f_T(\beta-tv)-\epsilon_n}{t}
 \leq u^Tv\leq
 \frac{f_T(\beta+tv)-f_T(\beta)+\epsilon_n}{t}.
\]
The profile loss is Lipschitz and is absolutely continuous along
these segments. Integrate the canonical subgradient along them;
the exceptional directional nondifferentiability points do not
affect the integral. Applying \eqref{eq:supp-gradient-rate} and the
population Hessian bound gives a directional error at most
$C(\delta_n+t+\epsilon_n/t)$. Set $t=\sqrt{\epsilon_n}$, with a
limit as $t\downarrow0$ when the gap is zero. This argument controls
the dual vector actually used by the search even at a sample
nondifferentiability point.
\end{proof}

The joint primal-dual certificate gives the required epsilon
subgradient. The Fenchel gap is the sum of the profile dual gap and
the nonnegative quadratic conjugacy gap for the selected coefficients.
Minimising the returned objective over the nuisance effects can only
reduce it. The remaining profile dual gap is therefore bounded by the
reported normalised joint gap.

\subsection{Greedy approximation and model size selection}

Put $\nu_n=\delta_n+n^{-1}+\sqrt{\epsilon_n}$. The prediction ridge
gradient has norm $O(n^{-1})$ on the considered coefficient ball.
At a restricted empirical fit the population gradient in the selected
span consequently has norm $O_p(\nu_n)$.

\begin{lemma}[Risk along the search path]\label{lem:supp-greedy}
Let $r_j=R_0(\widehat\beta_j)-R_0(\beta^\star)$ at search step $j$.
On the uniform gradient and sparse eigenvalue events,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-greedy-recursion}
 r_{j+1}\leq(1-c/s)r_j+C\nu_n^2,
 \qquad r_M\leq e^{-cM/s}r_0+Cs\nu_n^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Sparse Gram concentration bounds every eligible residualised column
norm above and away from zero. Let $b_{jl}$ be its normalised
coefficient direction. Resolve $\beta^\star-\widehat\beta_j$ into
the selected span and the residual directions of its at most $s$
omitted target columns. The selected component has norm at most a
constant times $\|\widehat\beta_j-\beta^\star\|_2$, and its gradient
product is at most $C\nu_n$ times that norm. The sum of the absolute
coefficients of the residual directions is at most
$C\sqrt{s}\|\widehat\beta_j-\beta^\star\|_2$. Convexity gives
$r_j\leq\nabla R_0(\widehat\beta_j)^T
(\widehat\beta_j-\beta^\star)$. Together with restricted curvature,
this yields
\[
 \max_{l\notin S_j}|\nabla R_0(\widehat\beta_j)^Tb_{jl}|
       \geq c\sqrt{r_j/s}-C\nu_n.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:supp-gradient} controls the computed dual correlations
in these directions. The chosen direction therefore has the same
lower bound with a different constant. A population step in that
direction decreases risk by its squared gradient divided by a fixed
upper-curvature constant.

For any enlarged selected set, compare its empirical fit with its
population restricted minimiser. Approximate empirical optimality,
the integrated gradient bound and the numerical ridge imply
\[
 \frac\kappa2\|\Delta\|_2^2
       \leq C(\delta_n+n^{-1})\|\Delta\|_2+\epsilon_n.
\]
Its population excess over that restricted minimum is
$O_p(\nu_n^2)$. Combining these inequalities proves the recursion.
Summing it proves the endpoint bound.
\end{proof}

The fits can be localised before this argument. Nuisance stationarity
and the bounded dual box imply $\|\gamma_i(\beta)\|_2\leq C$.
The population profile risk is bounded below by
$c\E m_i^{-1}\sum_j|X_{ij}^T\beta|
-\E m_i^{-1}\sum_j|Y_{ij}|-C$. The sparse small-ball bound and
the response moment condition place all restricted population
minimisers in a fixed ball. The uniform population Hessian Lipschitz
bound extends the assumed positive curvature to a fixed neighbourhood
of its coefficient ball, with a smaller positive lower constant.

For each selected support, consider a small fixed-radius sphere around
its restricted population minimiser. Integrating
Lemma~\ref{lem:supp-gradient} along radial segments bounds the
empirical loss increment minus its population increment by
$O_p(\delta_n)$ times the radius, uniformly over the supports.
The prediction ridge changes these increments by $O_p(n^{-1})$.
Population curvature therefore gives a positive constant objective
increase on every such sphere with probability tending to one.
Convexity and a vanishing primal-dual gap place the computed fits
inside these spheres. The preceding quadratic inequality then gives
their $O_p(\nu_n)$ distances from the restricted population minima.

\begin{lemma}[Heldout outcome comparison]\label{lem:supp-outcome-cv}
The selected inner path sets contain coefficient comparison vectors
with Euclidean error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-en}
 e_n=e^{-cM/(2s)}+\sqrt{s}\nu_n+\sqrt{\log(CM)/n}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a training-measurable candidate $\beta$, the heldout profile
loss difference from $\beta^\star$ is bounded by
$m^{-1}\sum_j|X_j^T(\beta-\beta^\star)|$. Its conditional
sub-Gaussian scale is at most $C\|\beta-\beta^\star\|_2$.
Population curvature and Young's inequality give, simultaneously
over the finite paths,
\[
 |(P_V-P)(\ell_0^{\mathrm{prof}}(\beta)
                  -\ell_0^{\mathrm{prof}}(\beta^\star))|
 \leq a\{R_0(\beta)-R_0(\beta^\star)\}
                       +C_a\log(CM/\delta)/|V|
\]
with probability at least $1-\delta$. The reference loss cancels
when the path size is selected. Compare the selected average risk
with the common endpoint in Lemma~\ref{lem:supp-greedy}. Every inner
fold has positive limiting weight, so the resulting risk order holds
for each selected inner fit. Restricted curvature gives
\eqref{eq:supp-en}. Their union retains these fitted comparison
vectors, and a population minimiser on the union has no larger risk.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Profile contrast selection}

The directional version of Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-oracle} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-directional-moments}
 \E|v^TH_{i,b}(\beta)w|^r
       \leq r!C^r b^{1-r}\|v\|_2^r\|w\|_2^r,
       \qquad r\geq2.
\end{equation}
Use coefficient nets on each sparse support and fixed-mesh nets on
the corresponding unit spheres. Their total logarithmic size is
$O(DL_n)$. Bernstein's inequality and the deterministic profile
Lipschitz bounds from Section~\ref{supp:primitive} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-operator-rate}
 \sup_{\beta,A}\|(P_T-P)H_{i,b}(\beta)_{AA}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
       =O_p(a_n),\quad
 a_n=\sqrt{DL_n/(nh)}+DL_n/(nh),\quad b\in\{h,h/2\}.
\end{equation}
For example, on the design event
$\max_{i,j,k}|X_{ij,k}|\leq C\sqrt{L_n}$, the entrywise Lipschitz
bound extends to a restricted bilinear form with only polynomial
factors in $D$, $L_n$ and $h^{-1}$. A coefficient mesh
$n^{-2}h^6/(D^2L_n^3)$ makes that extension negligible. Its
logarithmic cardinality still has order $DL_n$.

\begin{lemma}[Nodewise comparison and validation]\label{lem:supp-nodewise}
The selected profile graph sets contain contrasts $v_A$, with
$v_{A,k}=1$, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-rn}
 \|v_A-c_0\|_2=O_p(r_n),\quad
 r_n=(1+\sqrt{s_c})(a_n+e_n+h+n^{-1})
                      +\sqrt{\log(CM\log n)/(nh)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the curvature event in \eqref{eq:supp-operator-rate}, the
standardisation diagonals are bounded above and away from zero.
Work in these unit-diagonal coordinates. The nodewise gradient at
the sparse comparison $c_0$ is bounded by
$C(a_n+e_n+h)$, using the bandwidth expansion and population
curvature continuity. A penalty of at least a sufficiently large
multiple of this quantity gives the usual Lasso cone inequality.

Here the required compatibility follows from the stated sparse
eigenvalue bounds. Order the coefficients outside the comparison
support into blocks of size $m$. Their summed Euclidean norms are
bounded by the tail L1 norm divided by $\sqrt m$. The triangle
inequality in the positive quadratic norm shows that the leading
block's lower eigenvalue dominates the tail when $m$ is a sufficiently
large fixed multiple of $s_c$. This yields a lower bound on the
prediction norm throughout the cone. The basic inequality then gives
squared prediction error $Cs_c(a_n+e_n+h)^2$.

The KKT equations also bound support size. On any subset of active
coordinates, the penalty norm, after subtracting the comparison
gradient, is at most the sparse upper eigenvalue times the prediction
error. Choosing an active subset larger than a sufficiently large
multiple of $s_c$ contradicts the preceding error bound. The declared
graph budget therefore admits this candidate.

The penalty anchors of the inner fits and both bandwidths differ from
their common population anchor by $O_p(a_n+e_n+h)$. If that anchor is
of this order, the zero graph is a comparison of the stated accuracy.
Otherwise the anchors are comparable and the geometric ratio grid
contains a common eligible penalty of the required order. Its lower
endpoint is smaller than that order. The bounded ratio spacing keeps
the comparison constants fixed.

Post Lasso quadratic fitting on the outcome and graph union preserves
the comparison. Its population restricted minimiser has no larger
quadratic risk than the Lasso comparison. The restricted normal
equations and \eqref{eq:supp-operator-rate} bound the empirical
refit's error from that minimiser by $C(a_n+n^{-1})$.

For validation, write $\Delta=c-c_0$ and
$\delta=e_n+h+n^{-1}$. Population curvature gives
\[
 \E\{\ell_{graph}(c)-\ell_{graph}(c_0)\}
       \geq\kappa\|\Delta\|_2^2-C\delta\|\Delta\|_2.
\]
Add a common sufficiently large multiple of $\delta^2$. This shifted
excess is nonnegative and bounds $\|\Delta\|_2^2$. By
\eqref{eq:supp-directional-moments}, the variance of a heldout loss
difference is at most $C/h$ times the shifted excess. Bernstein's
inequality therefore gives an oracle remainder
$C\log(CM\log n)/(nh)$ over the possible outcome path sizes and
nodewise ratios. The reference loss and shift cancel in ratio
selection. Taking a union over outcome sizes permits the
data-selected size to enter this step. Curvature converts the
resulting risk bound to \eqref{eq:supp-rn}. Combining inner supports
retains each comparison contrast. The additional design regression
does not remove any of them.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The selected model parameter}

\begin{proposition}[Selected target displacement]\label{prop:supp-selected-target}
Suppose $A$ contains the comparison vectors of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:supp-outcome-cv} and \ref{lem:supp-nodewise}.
Then
\[
 \|\beta_A^P-\beta^\star\|_2=O_p(e_n),\qquad
 |\beta_{A,k}^P-\beta_k^\star|=O_p(e_n^2+r_ne_n).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Population minimisation on $A$ cannot increase the comparison risk.
Restricted curvature gives the Euclidean bound. If localisation is
needed, convexity and the curvature on the boundary of the local
ball force any minimiser with this small risk into its interior.
Restricted stationarity gives $v_A^T\nabla R_0(\beta_A^P)=0$.
For $\Delta_A=\beta_A^P-\beta^\star$, Taylor expansion gives
\[
 0=J_0\Delta_{A,k}+(v_A-c_0)^TH_0(\beta^\star)\Delta_A
                              +O(\|\Delta_A\|_2^2).
\]
The sparse operator bound controls the middle term by $Cr_ne_n$,
and $J_0$ is bounded away from zero.
\end{proof}

For the Gaussian statement, conditional on $(Z,m)$ use
\[
 X_{A^c}=X_A\Sigma_{AA}^{-1}\Sigma_{A,A^c}+E_A,
 \qquad E_A\ \text{independent of }X_A.
\]
Substitution into the outcome shows that at
$\beta_A^0+\Sigma_{AA}^{-1}\Sigma_{A,A^c}\beta_{A^c}^0$ the
profile residual vector is independent of the centred selected
design. Its expected complete profile gradient is zero at every
bandwidth, and identification gives the stated selected target.
If $v=\Sigma^{-1}e_k$ is supported on $A$, its block equations give
$v_A=\Sigma_{AA}^{-1}e_{k,A}$ and
$\Sigma_{A^c,A}v_A=0$. Thus
$e_{k,A}^T\Sigma_{AA}^{-1}\Sigma_{A,A^c}=0$, proving exact
preservation of the target coordinate.

\subsection{The selected coefficient expansion}

The population density proof can be applied to arbitrary sparse
design contrasts. The directional moment condition bounds the
remainder in Euclidean norm and the Hessian remainder in restricted
operator norm. At a selected population minimiser, the implicit
equation consequently gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-root-expansion}
 \beta_{A,b}^P=\beta_A^P
       -b\{H_0(\beta_A^P)_{AA}\}^{-1}B_{1,A}(\beta_A^P)+O(b^2).
\end{equation}
The same set at both bandwidths has the same coefficient of $b$.
Pairing \eqref{eq:supp-root-expansion} and using
Proposition~\ref{prop:supp-selected-target} gives target error
$O_p(h^2+e_n^2+r_ne_n)$.

Conditional on the selection sample, the estimation observations are
iid and $A$ is fixed. A coefficient net within that selected space
has logarithmic size $O(d_n\log n)$. The gradient and curvature
arguments give
\[
 u_n=O\!\left(\sqrt{d_n\log n/n}\right),\qquad
 b_n=O\!\left(\sqrt{d_n\log n/(nh)}+d_n\log n/(nh)\right)
\]
for the coefficient error and local curvature fluctuation.
Convexity localises the empirical minimiser around $\beta_{A,b}^P$.
Taylor expansion of its selected score equations gives a remainder
of order $u_nb_n+u_n^2+n_f^{-2}$, together with the numerical
residual. The condition $(d_n\log n)^3/(nh)\to0$ makes this remainder
$o(n^{-1/2})$.

The same condition controls fitted second moments. At a fixed
direction, the curvature second moment is $O(1/h)$ and its fourth
moment is $O(1/h^3)$. Truncating the selected design norm at
$C\sqrt{d_n\log n}$ and taking coefficient and sphere nets gives
deviations bounded by
\[
 C\sqrt{d_n\log n/(nh^3)}
            +C d_n^3(\log n)^3/(nh^2).
\]
They are $o(1/h)$ under the stated condition. Integration along a
coefficient segment therefore bounds the empirical squared score
change by its squared coefficient distance divided by $h$.

\subsection{Influence representation and variance}

For each estimation fold and bandwidth, expand its selected target
estimate about $\beta_{A,b}^P$. Let $\omega_{A,b}$ be the population
selected inverse row. The comparison contrast and population
continuity imply
\[
 \|\omega_{A,b}-\omega_0\|_2=O_p(r_n+e_n+h).
\]
To see this, $\omega_0$ has outside-$A$ norm at most $Cr_n$ because
the comparison contrast is supported on $A$. Subtract the selected
normal equations from $H_0(\beta^\star)\omega_0=e_k$. Restricted
curvature bounds the inside-$A$ difference by $C(r_n+e_n+h)$.

After centring each population score at its own mean, the directional
moment bound controls the conditional mean squared difference between
the selected population contribution and
$-\omega_0^T\{g_{i,b}(\beta^\star)-m_b\}$ by
\[
 C(e_n^2/h+r_n^2+h).
\]
The term $h$ includes the bandwidth displacement of the population
selected coefficient. Conditional Chebyshev bounds make the
corresponding root sample size scaled averages negligible. This
conditioning is applied separately to each fold; no joint
independence of both selected sets from the data is used.

The two bandwidth expansions, with weights $(2,-1)$, give
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_k-\beta_k^\star)
       =n^{-1/2}\sum_i\xi_{i,h}+o_p(1)
\]
when $\sqrt n(h^2+e_n^2+r_ne_n)\to0$. Fold weights $n_f/n$
assign each estimation cluster weight $1/n$. Uniform moments above
order two and the nondegenerate triangular-array variance yield the
central limit theorem.

For the sandwich, the fitted inverse rows converge to their
population counterparts. The fitted second-moment bound and
$e_n^2/h+r_n^2\to0$ make the average squared difference between
the recorded paired cluster contributions and their population
versions vanish. The empirical covariance of fixed population scores
has bounded restricted operator norm under the design contrast
moment condition. Cauchy--Schwarz controls the remaining cross terms.
Centring within a fixed number of proportional folds preserves the
limit, giving
\[
 n\widehat s_k^2/\sigma_h^2\longrightarrow_p1.
\]
Slutsky's theorem proves the stated normal interval. When the
projected bandwidth scores also converge uniformly in mean square,
the influence can be written as
$-\omega_0^Tg_{i,0}(\beta^\star)$. The triangular-array formulation
already supplies the studentised conclusion.

For bounded sparsities, $M\asymp(\log n)^3$ and $h=n^{-3/10}$,
the condition $D\log(2np)/\sqrt{nh}\to0$ gives the displayed
high-dimensional regime in the article. The other requirements follow
from $\sqrt n h^2=n^{-1/10}$ and the polylogarithmic selected
dimension. If the model is fixed independently of its estimation
outcomes, the selection target term is absent and the same proof
uses a single estimation sample.
