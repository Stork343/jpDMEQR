\section{Longitudinal SLE transcriptomic study}\label{sec:application}

\subsection{Cohort and scientific question}\label{subsec:data-source}

GEO accession GSE65391 records repeated whole-blood expression and
clinical measurements in paediatric systemic lupus erythematosus
\cite{banchereau2016}. The source study followed 158 patients and
obtained 924 SLE expression samples on the Illumina HumanHT-12 V4.0
array. The present outcome is log-transformed complement C3 at the
same visit as the expression measurement. Requiring positive observed
C3, usable expression and at least two visits retains
\SLEEligibleSubjects{} subjects and \SLEEligibleVisits{} visits.
Table~\ref{tab:data-audit} gives the observed cohort construction.

The question is whether immune expression associations with C3 vary
across its distribution after clinical adjustment. Subjects are the
independent sampling units. The complete profile averages within each
subject before averaging across subjects, so visit-rich subjects
receive the same total weight as other subjects. The analysis uses
$\tau=0.25,0.50,0.75$, with the lower decile examined at bandwidths
that satisfy its local outcome-information checks.

\subsection{Expression construction and adjustment}\label{subsec:preprocessing}

The reference expression input is the processed GEO Series Matrix.
Controls, unannotated probes and probes with ambiguous gene mappings
are removed. Among probes mapping uniquely to the same gene, the
training sample selects the probe with the largest median absolute
deviation. Training visits also determine feature filtering and
standardisation. Prediction considers 5000 genes, with 2000 and
10000 examined as sensitivity values. Each inner training set
repeats these transformations using its own subjects.

Model A adjusts for centred follow-up time, age, sex, race or ancestry
categories, treatment indicators and source batch. Model B adds
contemporaneous SLEDAI. Its low-complement component assigns two
points when C3, C4 or CH50 is below the laboratory reference range
\cite{torontoSLEDAI2K}. Model B consequently addresses a conditional
association with a clinical composite that partly incorporates
complement. The nuisance design contains a subject intercept; a
centred-time slope is included in sensitivity calculations.

Two expression-processing sensitivities use the same biological
visits. One begins with the official non-normalised R1/R2 arrays,
uses healthy technical pairs to calibrate their releases and estimates
a quantile-normalisation target within each SLE training sample.
The other takes the median across uniquely mapped probes for a gene.
Supporting Information specifies these transformations and their
repetition within validation and subject deletion.

\subsection{Module parameters and uncertainty}\label{subsec:estimands}

Five immune modules from the public G2 repertoire
\cite{chaussabel2014,banchereau2016} were fixed before fitting C3
associations. M1.2, M3.4 and M5.12 represent interferon responses;
M4.11 represents plasma cells, and M5.15 represents neutrophils.
Table~\ref{tab:immune-modules} gives their measured membership.
The first four scores average their complete gene sets, whereas
M5.15 averages its original probe set. Each score is centred and
divided by its training-sample standard deviation. The resulting
clinical and module designs have \SLEModelADimension{} coordinates
in Model A and \SLEModelBDimension{} in Model B.

Each module coefficient is a coordinate of the population complete
profile for that clinical and module design, with fixed $\Lambda=I$.
The fixed-design case of Section~\ref{subsec:paired-fit} fits both
convex profiles at $h=n^{-3/10}$ and $h/2$ and combines their
coefficients. The cluster-score covariance uses both fitted inverse
rows and the within-subject covariance of their score contributions.
A second calculation deletes each complete subject, repeats
preprocessing and estimation, and forms the delete-one covariance.
It holds the full-sample bandwidth fixed and retains each repeated
estimator's sample-standardised module units.

The score interval describes variation conditional on the fitted
feature construction. The complete-workflow interval also includes
variation from probe selection, centring and standardisation. Each
procedure uses Bonferroni allocation across the 30 module coordinates
formed by two models and three quantiles. The lower-decile family
contains ten coordinates. All reference and sensitivity workflows
complete their full-sample fit and every subject deletion.

\subsection{Immune module associations}\label{subsec:module-results}

The M4.11 plasma-cell coefficient is negative in all model and
quantile combinations. In Model A, its lower-quartile estimate is
\JointPlasmaALowerEstimate{}, with simultaneous score interval
\JointPlasmaALowerScoreInterval{} and complete-workflow interval
\JointPlasmaALowerWorkflowInterval{}. Its median estimate is
\JointPlasmaAMedianEstimate{}, with intervals
\JointPlasmaAMedianScoreInterval{} and
\JointPlasmaAMedianWorkflowInterval{}. Model B gives median estimate
\JointPlasmaBMedianEstimate{} and corresponding intervals
\JointPlasmaBMedianScoreInterval{} and
\JointPlasmaBMedianWorkflowInterval{}.

Figure~\ref{fig:joint-modules} displays the full reference family.
The upper-quartile plasma-cell intervals include zero, as do the
interferon and neutrophil intervals across the three quantiles.
Both processing sensitivities retain the negative plasma-cell
associations at the lower quartile and median in Model A and at
the median in Model B under both uncertainty calculations.
Their numerical intervals and the working-penalty, bandwidth and
random-slope sensitivities appear in Supporting Information.

\subsection{Prediction for heldout subjects}\label{subsec:application-reporting}

Five repetitions of five-fold validation assign complete subjects to
folds. Within each outer training sample, five inner subject folds
select the outcome-search size and the nuisance penalty from
$\{0.5I,I,2I\}$ by equal-subject pinball loss. Clinical covariates
and the five modules remain included throughout the search.
The selected unsmoothed joint fit supplies the fixed-part predictor
for heldout subjects. Its statistical task is prediction with a
training-selected working matrix; the module intervals retain their
fixed matrix.

All \JointPredictionFits{} prediction fits across the reference,
feature-count, random-slope and processing settings are complete.
Each setting predicts every retained subject in each of the five
repetitions. Table~\ref{tab:joint-prediction} reports equal-subject
pinball loss and calibration, the proportion of outcomes below the
predicted quantile. The upper-quartile fixed-part predictions have
calibration below their nominal quantile in both adjustment models.
Persistent subject variation therefore remains relevant when
interpreting these predictions for a previously unobserved subject.

Sensitivity comparisons average the five loss differences within a
subject and then across subjects. Complete-subject resampling gives
a 95\% range conditional on the fitted fold models. Supporting
Information reports these comparisons and the available paired
predictions from pooled quantile Lasso, longitudinal SCAD estimating
equations and a low-dimensional linear quantile mixed model
\cite{zu2023,geraci2014jss}, together with their actual fold counts.

\subsection{Independent exploratory gene designs}\label{subsec:exploratory-results}

Ten subject splits, each used in both role assignments, separate
gene selection from inference. The selection group supplies all
preprocessing and exactly three additions along the joint profile
search path. The other group fits the complete clinical and
three-gene design at both bandwidths. Each interval addresses the
profile parameter of its own selected design.

Every role completes selection and inference under all three
processing routes. Under reference processing, the lower-quartile
search selects \JointLowerGeneA{} in \JointLowerGeneCountA{} of
20 Model A assignments and \JointLowerGeneB{} in
\JointLowerGeneCountB{} Model B assignments. The selections at other
quantiles are more dispersed. Supporting Information reports their
frequencies and descriptive coefficient distributions across the
designs in which each gene is selected.
