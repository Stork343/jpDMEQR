\subsection{Metadata and outcome information}

Source phenotype fields and their parsed versions are retained
separately. Subject and visit identifiers define biological visits.
One record with an unavailable visit number has a unique observed
subject and cumulative-time combination. A source replicate flag
is interpreted alongside the observed record relationships; a
flagged SLE measurement remains eligible when it is the sole
observed biological record at that subject and visit.

Positive C3 values, usable expression and repeated measurements
yield \SLEEligibleSubjects{} subjects and \SLEEligibleVisits{}
visits. Both batches contain observations in all four outcome
regions defined by the three analysis quantiles. The recorded
SLEDAI total is used in Model B. Its low-complement component
includes C3, C4 or CH50 below the laboratory reference range
\cite{torontoSLEDAI2K}.

Local outcome information is checked at $\tau=0.25,0.50,0.75$ and
bandwidth multipliers $0.75,1,1.25$. Each of 10000 subject bootstrap
samples re-estimates the empirical quantile and the equal-subject
mean kernel weight. The nine combinations share one-sided error
allocation $0.05/9$. Their lower percentile bounds are positive,
and local counts and Kish effective subject counts exceed
$\lfloor n/\log(\max(n,3))\rfloor+1$. The lower-decile calculations
use a separate family and the eligible bandwidth multipliers
$1$ and $1.25$. Each actual profile fit also checks its selected
curvature and final score residual.

\subsection{Expression transformations}

The processed Series Matrix supplies the reference normalised
expression. Official GPL10558 annotation retains probe IDs, gene
symbols and Gene IDs. Historical symbols with a unique Gene ID
mapping retain their source identifiers. Module membership and
measurement units are fixed independently of C3 associations.
All required members have observed expression.

The four gene-based modules retain the uniquely mapped probe with
largest training-sample median absolute deviation for each member.
The fifth module retains its fixed original probe representation.
Each module averages its members before training-sample centring
and standardisation. Gene prediction similarly uses training-sample
probe selection, feature filtering and scaling. Complete subject
roles determine the training observations at every stage.

For the raw-array sensitivity, detection-p-value normexp correction
uses \texttt{limma::nec} with offset 16 and
\texttt{robust=FALSE}. Each array's background parameters depend on
its own measurements. Healthy technical pairs spanning R1 and R2
provide a per-probe arithmetic-mean R2/R1 ratio that multiplies R1
measurements. These healthy references are disjoint from the SLE
cohort. A quantile-normalisation target estimated on the current
SLE training visits is applied to validation arrays and followed
by $\log_2$ transformation. Every inner training role and subject
deletion estimates its own target.

The probe-aggregation sensitivity takes the median of uniquely
mapped probes for each gene and visit. M5.15 keeps its original
probe representation. All processing routes use the same eligible
visits, module membership and frozen subject assignments.

\subsection{Complete module workflows}

The clinical and module designs contain \SLEModelADimension{}
coordinates in Model A and \SLEModelBDimension{} in Model B.
For each setting, the fixed-design interface jointly fits the
complete selected profile at $h$ and $h/2$. It retains the separate
nuisance solutions, Hessian inverses and subject scores, then forms
the paired coefficient and covariance.

Let $a(D)$ denote the sample-fitted expression and clinical
transformations and write
\[
 T(D)=2\widehat\beta_{h/2}\{D;a(D),\Lambda\}
                      -\widehat\beta_h\{D;a(D),\Lambda\}.
\]
For $T_{-i}=T(D_{-i})$ and
$\overline T_-=n^{-1}\sum_iT_{-i}$, the complete-workflow covariance
is
\[
 \widehat V_T=\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_i
       (T_{-i}-\overline T_-)(T_{-i}-\overline T_-)^T.
\]
Each deletion repeats probe selection, centring and standardisation
while holding $h$ at its full-sample value. The repeated
coefficients retain their sample-standardised units. For a fixed
raw-scale module with slope $\theta(D)$ and estimated scale $s_D$,
the corresponding coefficient is $s_D\theta(D)$. Its variation
therefore includes estimation of that scale.

For a random slope, changing the time centre also changes the
nuisance coordinates. If $Z_D=Z_{ref}R_D$, the fixed matrix
$\Lambda$ in the sample's coordinates corresponds to
$R_D^{-T}\Lambda R_D^{-1}$ in reference coordinates. Recomputing
the complete deletion estimator retains the variation from the
estimated centre.

The reference interval family has $K=30$ module coordinates, with
critical value $z_{1-0.05/(2K)}$ for each interval procedure. Each
working-penalty, bandwidth, random-slope or processing sensitivity
uses its own declared family. At $\tau=0.10$, $K=10$.
The result builder calculates every simultaneous interval from its
saved coefficient and standard error using this family definition.
The \JointModuleWorkflows{} reference and sensitivity workflows
each complete the full fit and all \SLEEligibleSubjects{} deletions,
giving \JointModulePairedFits{} paired coefficient fits.

\subsection{Repeated subject validation}

Five repetitions of five outer subject folds define prediction
roles. Each outer role has five inner subject folds. The outcome
search considers additions from zero through the common inner
budget $\lfloor r/\log r\rfloor$, with clinical and module
coordinates always included. Inner validation selects this size
and $\lambda\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, setting $\Lambda=\lambda I$.
Loss is the fixed-part pinball loss averaged within subjects and
then over the heldout training subjects. Ties follow the declared
candidate order. The chosen size and matrix are fitted on the
complete outer training sample.

The reference processing includes 5000 candidate genes, feature
counts 2000 and 10000, and a 5000-gene random-slope sensitivity.
Both alternative processing routes use 5000 candidates and a
random intercept. Across two clinical models and three quantiles,
these specifications give \JointPredictionFits{} complete outer
fits. The final random-slope records use the prescribed quadratic
solver in Section~\ref{supp:computation}; retained numerical
attempts are separately accounted for in the replication files.

For each sensitivity, a subject supplies a vector of five
out-of-fold loss differences relative to reference processing with
5000 genes and a random intercept. The reported difference averages
within this vector and then equally across all 129 subjects.
Ten thousand multinomial resamples of the subject vectors give
the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. The same weights are used for
every sensitivity. These are conditional resampling ranges for the
saved fold models; training-sample variation is held fixed.

The recorded published-method comparisons supply six completed
outer computations for each of pooled quantile Lasso, QGEE-SCAD and LQMM.

The table describes the available subject subset.

The LQMM entries retain the returned package fits and their

training-fold choice among its declared screening dimensions and

official \texttt{lqmm} implementation on the clinical and module
design with a random intercept. The replication records preserve
their package versions, selected settings and convergence output.

\subsection{Independent exploratory gene inference}

Each of ten subject splits is used in both role assignments.
Selection-group preprocessing supplies 5000 candidate genes.
Clinical covariates remain included and three steps of the joint
outcome search select exactly three genes, with $\Lambda=I$.
The independent group estimates the resulting clinical and
three-gene profile with bandwidth equal to its cluster count to
power $-3/10$. The interval refers to that role-specific selected
population parameter.

All \JointGeneRoles{} model, quantile, processing and role combinations
complete independent inference, giving \JointGeneIntervals{}
coordinate intervals.
Table~\ref{tab:joint-genes} reports the three most frequent selected
identifiers within each processing route, model and quantile.
Frequency has denominator 20 role assignments. Coefficient medians
and interquartile ranges condition on selection and describe the
observed selected designs. Every individual interval remains in
the replication records with its exact selection and inference
subjects.
