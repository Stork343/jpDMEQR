\section{Conditions and notation for selected profile models}\label{supp:conditions}

Expectations refer to independent clusters within each row of a
triangular array. The dimension and distribution may depend on $n$.
For a cluster set $T$, $P_T$ denotes its empirical average. Define
\[
 R_b(\beta)=\E\ell_{i,b}^{\mathrm{prof}}(\beta),\quad
 m_b(\beta)=\E g_{i,b}(\beta),\quad
 \mathcal H_b(\beta)=\E H_{i,b}(\beta),\quad
 H_0(\beta)=\nabla^2R_0(\beta).
\]
The target is $\beta^\star=\arg\min R_0(\beta)$. For a set $A$,
$\beta_A^P$ minimises $R_0$ under $\beta_{A^c}=0$, and
$\beta_{A,b}^P$ minimises $R_b$ under the same restriction. These
vectors are embedded in the complete coefficient space.

\subsection{Primitive distribution assumptions}

Cluster sizes are bounded, $\Lambda$ is fixed with eigenvalues in a
positive compact interval, and $Z$ is bounded. The kernel assumptions
and joint density section bounds are those in
Section~\ref{supp:primitive}. The conditional density has integrated
derivatives through order three. The nuisance dimension is fixed, and
the nuisance design may have repeated rows or deficient rank.

Let
\[
 \Theta_{D,R}^{(2)}=\{\beta:\|\beta\|_0\leq D,\ \|\beta\|_2\leq R\}.
\]
Conditional on $(Z,m)$, a contrast supported on the relevant sparse
sets has sub-Gaussian norm bounded by $C\|v\|_2$. This condition also
holds on fixed multiples of these sets used to compare coefficients
and contrasts. Sparse design Gram eigenvalues lie between positive
constants. The population profile Hessian satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-pop-curvature}
 \kappa\|v\|_2^2\leq v^TH_0(\beta)v\leq C\|v\|_2^2
\end{equation}
on the stated sparse ball and enlarged supports. The corresponding
third directional derivative is bounded by
$C\|u\|_2\|v\|_2\|w\|_2$. The upper derivative bounds follow from
the density sections and design contrast moments; the lower curvature
bound is an identification condition.

The average absolute response has bounded expectation. A ball
containing the sparse search fits can be obtained from this moment
condition and sparse small-ball concentration, as shown below.
All population minimisers used in a local expansion are in the
interior of a ball on which \eqref{eq:supp-pop-curvature} holds.

\subsection{Sparse comparison vectors}

The target has at most $s$ nonzero coordinates and bounded Euclidean
norm, with $s\geq1$. The always-included set $\mathcal U$ has fixed
size. For a requested coordinate $k$, suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-contrast}
 c_{0k,k}=1,\qquad H_0(\beta^\star)c_{0k}=J_{0k}e_k,
 \qquad 0<c\leq J_{0k}\leq C.
\end{equation}
The contrast has support size at most $s_c$ and bounded L1 norm.
Write $\omega_{0k}=c_{0k}/J_{0k}$. For simplicity the proofs suppress
the coordinate subscript when only one target is considered.

Let $M$ bound the unadjusted inner capacities
$\min\{p,\lfloor r/\log r\rfloor,\lceil(\log r)^3\rceil\}$.
The smallest capacity has the same order. The final selected set has
at most $d_n\leq|\mathcal U|+7M+1$ coordinates. In uniform learning
bounds use $D=7M+s+s_c+|\mathcal U|+2$ and, when needed, a fixed
multiple of $D$. The graph budget is large enough to contain the
sparse Lasso comparison support. Sparse eigenvalue bounds on a
sufficiently large fixed multiple of $s_c$ imply compatibility on
the resulting Lasso cones.

The learning rates are stated in the regime $a_n+\nu_n\to0$, with
$a_n$ and $\nu_n$ defined in the article. This condition supplies
uniform curvature and localisation of the empirical selected fits.

The number of outer and inner folds is fixed and their sizes are
proportional to $n$. Target identities are fixed independently of their
estimation outcomes. All feature definitions and units are common to
the fold estimates that are combined. Role-specific exploratory
models are retained separately when their definitions differ.

\subsection{Numerical accuracy and variance}

An initial fit has a normalised primal-dual gap at most $\epsilon_n$.
Nodewise numerical residuals and support thresholds are negligible
relative to the stated contrast rate. The final selected fits solve
their score equations with an error contributing $o_p(n^{-1/2})$ to
the target coordinate. Their prediction ridge has order $n_f^{-2}$.
The theoretical estimators are the certified minimisers; an exhausted
numerical budget yields a recorded failed attempt.

At $m_b=m_b(\beta^\star)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-oracle-influence}
 \xi_{i,h}=-\omega_0^T
 [2\{g_{i,h/2}(\beta^\star)-m_{h/2}\}
                    -\{g_{i,h}(\beta^\star)-m_h\}].
\end{equation}
Its variance $\sigma_h^2$ is bounded above and away from zero. Bounded
dual scores and the design contrast condition give uniform moments
above order two. No limit of the triangular-array variance is needed
for the studentised conclusion.
