\section{Computation and numerical verification}\label{supp:computation}

\subsection{Exact unsmoothed cluster profiles}

For an intercept, nuisance stationarity is a monotone piecewise
linear equation. Its slope between residual knots is the positive
ridge coefficient. Sorting the knots determines either the interval
containing its zero or the knot at which the subgradient contains
zero. A box-feasible dual vector is recovered at the zero residuals,
and the complete primal-dual gap is checked.

For an intercept and slope, whiten the nuisance penalty by
$\theta=\Lambda^{1/2}\gamma$ and $V=Z\Lambda^{-1/2}$. The cluster
objective becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-finite-profile}
 \tfrac12\|\theta\|_2^2+
            m^{-1}\sum_j\rho_\tau(u_j-v_j^T\theta).
\end{equation}
Residual-zero lines partition the plane into cells. Within a cell,
the optimum is the stationary point of a quadratic with fixed
residual signs. On any residual-zero line, an orthogonal
parameterisation reduces the restricted objective to the scalar
problem just described.

The finite candidate construction consists of those line minimisers
and the stationary points of their adjacent cells. A global minimum
on a residual-zero line is its restricted minimum. If the global
minimum is inside a cell, project it onto a nearest supporting line.
Choosing a line with smallest distance keeps this projection in the
cell closure. Its tangential derivative is zero, so it is the
minimiser on that line. The original cell is adjacent to this point
and its stationary point is examined. Under the distinct-time
design, active rows span the required nuisance directions. Box
feasibility and the complete gap verify every accepted candidate,
including active residual ties.

Bounded cluster size makes this a bounded-dimensional calculation.
The implementation retains its exact gap and terminates an attempt
if no candidate satisfies the numerical certificate.

\subsection{Joint convex optimisation in an orthogonal basis}

On a training sample with $r$ clusters, let
$w_{ij}=1/(r m_i)$ and write
$\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt w)X_S=Q_SR_S$. Put
$B_S=\operatorname{diag}(w^{-1/2})Q_S$ and $a=R_S\beta_S$.
Multiplying the selected criterion by $r$ gives a saddle problem
with primal quadratic
\[
 G(a,\gamma)=\tfrac12\|a\|_2^2
                       +\tfrac12\sum_i\gamma_i^T\Lambda\gamma_i
\]
and dual box
$\mathcal C=\prod_{ij}[(\tau-1)/m_i,\tau/m_i]$.
For a feasible dual vector $u$, its value is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-joint-dual}
 D_S(u)=Y^Tu-\tfrac12\|B_S^Tu\|_2^2
       -\tfrac12\sum_i(Z_i^Tu_i)^T\Lambda^{-1}(Z_i^Tu_i).
\end{equation}
The proximal maps are a scalar ridge update for $a$, small positive
linear systems for the nuisance vectors, and projection onto
$\mathcal C$ for the dual variables. The linesearch of
\citeA{malitsky2018} is applied to the combined fixed and nuisance
operator. Its accepted steps and the gap in
\eqref{eq:supp-joint-dual} are recorded.

If a new weighted orthogonal column $b_j$ is added, the feasible dual
value changes by $-(b_j^Tu)^2/2$. Thus a current normalised gap
$\epsilon_S$ bounds the possible improvement by
\[
 \epsilon_S+(b_j^Tu)^2/(2r).
\]
The search orders these conditional dual correlations. Reorthogonalised
Gram--Schmidt updates preserve the existing basis and allow warm
initialisation of the enlarged convex problem.

A change of nuisance coordinates can balance observed units.
For a positive diagonal matrix $S$, replace $Z$ by $ZS^{-1}$,
$\gamma$ by $S\gamma$ and $\Lambda$ by $S^{-1}\Lambda S^{-1}$.
Both the predictor and the nuisance penalty are unchanged. The
application prediction solver uses cluster-weighted RMS scales for
this numerical transformation, which is useful when time is recorded
in days.

For the random-slope prediction calculation, we use a sparse
quadratic programme on the same selected orthogonal basis. Introduce
nonnegative residual parts $r^+,r^-$ with
$B_Sa+Z\gamma+r^+-r^-=Y$. The objective is
\[
 \tfrac12\|a\|_2^2+\tfrac12\sum_i\gamma_i^T\Lambda\gamma_i
       +\sum_{ij}\{\tau r_{ij}^++(1-\tau)r_{ij}^-\}/m_i.
\]
The Clarabel implementation \cite{goulart2026} solves its equality and nonnegativity
constraints. Its equality dual variables supply a feasible box
score after projection. We recompute the check-loss primal and
\eqref{eq:supp-joint-dual} directly and require the same normalised
gap tolerance. This solver is specified for every random-slope
prediction fit. Eighteen deterministic comparisons against the
primal-dual solver check the common objective and the prediction
distance implied by their gap certificates.

\subsection{Positive curvature estimation fits}

Final coefficient fitting uses the complete smooth profile at each
bandwidth. The Newton matrix is its selected Schur Hessian plus the
declared numerical prediction ridge. Backtracking evaluates the
complete objective. The two fits retain separate nuisance solutions
and inverse rows; their cluster contributions are combined before
forming the covariance matrix.

The initial search gap is at most
$\min(10^{-8},r^{-3})$ times the objective scale. The final selected
gradient tolerance is $\min(10^{-8},n_f^{-2})$, accompanied by a
coefficient-step tolerance of $\min(10^{-7},n_f^{-3/2})$.
The nuisance gradient tolerance is
$\min(10^{-10},n_f^{-3})$. Tolerances specify numerical accuracy and
are not selected from interval coverage. Numerical budgets are
separate from these accuracy requirements.

The verification suite checks profile derivatives and the Schur
identity on deterministic generated data. Weighted fits are compared
with explicitly duplicated cluster records. Further checks cover the
two-bandwidth covariance, changes of coefficient units and observation
order. The fixed-design interface is compared coordinate by coordinate
with the same selected convex problems. Each simulated or empirical
attempt preserves its outcome and diagnostics.

\subsection{Prediction on independent subjects}

All adaptive expression transformations are fitted on the relevant
training subjects. The predictive learner uses the same unsmoothed
joint profile search with a budget for additional variables of
$\min\{p-|\mathcal U|,\lfloor r/\log r\rfloor\}$.
Prespecified clinical and module coordinates remain included.
Five inner subject folds select the search size and working nuisance
penalty from $\{0.5I,I,2I\}$. Their loss is the average heldout
pinball loss of the fixed-part prediction, averaged within subjects.
The chosen size and penalty are fitted on the complete outer training
sample. Predictions use $X_{new}^T\widehat\beta^{pred}$.

The predictive tuning task is distinct from coordinate inference
with a fixed working matrix. In both tasks the complete criterion
and declared subject roles determine which observations enter each
fit. The observed outcomes of heldout subjects do not enter variable
search, probe selection or tuning.

\subsection{Published comparison procedures}

The simulation comparisons use pooled quantile Lasso, the smoothed
quantile estimator of \citeA{yan2023}, linear quantile mixed models
\cite{geraci2014,geraci2014jss}, the resampling-adjusted estimator of
\citeA{battagliola2022}, the double-penalised criterion of
\citeA{li2020}, longitudinal SCAD estimating equations
\cite{zu2023}, and the quadratic inference function of
\citeA{bhattacharya2026}. Each procedure retains its own objective
and uncertainty calculation. The registry specifies the settings
in which each is evaluated.

Pooled quantile Lasso uses an unpenalised intercept and the raw-sum
check-loss objective. Five subject folds select a penalty multiplier
from $\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$ applied to
$\{\tau(1-\tau)N\log p\}^{1/2}$, where $N$ is the observation
count in that training role. Its dual linear programme constrains
each observation score to $[\tau-1,\tau]$ and each penalised
coordinate of $X^Tu$ to the corresponding penalty interval.
The returned coefficients are checked through the complete primal
and dual objectives and all coordinate inequalities.

The smoothed quantile comparison uses Gaussian convolution,
bandwidth $\max\{\sqrt{\tau(1-\tau)\log p}\,N^{-3/10},0.05\}$,
and the source one-step correction and iid variance. Its initial
L1 penalty is selected by five subject folds. Precision tuning
uses five additional subject folds; each training fold repeats
the initial penalty selection. For every precision candidate,
the complete CLIME matrix solves the source rowwise L1 programmes
and applies its pairwise minimum symmetrisation. Validation loss
is $p^{-1}\|H_{\mathrm{validation}}W-I\|_F^2$. The source leaves
this validation criterion and fold count unspecified; these are
the fixed choices used here. The multiplier grid is
$\{0.02,0.05,0.10,0.25,0.50,1,2,4\}$, with tolerances restricted
to $(0,1)$. In a role containing $r$ clusters, these multipliers
scale $\{\log(p)/(r b_r)\}^{1/2}+b_r^2$, where $b_r=r^{-3/10}$.
This grid anchor is recalculated within each training role.

Precision computation uses sparse candidate solutions from a
parametric simplex path or a quadratic working problem. A candidate
is accepted only after satisfying the original complete CLIME
inequalities and a primal-dual optimality check. Linear-programme
refinement retains all coefficient variables. Because fold losses
are nonnegative, their accumulated sum divided by five is a lower
bound on the complete validation loss. This bound permits exact
elimination of a candidate once it exceeds the best complete
candidate, including the tie tolerance. The selected candidate
has all five fold losses. Numerical failures and their affected
candidates remain recorded.

LQMM and the resampling-adjusted procedure use the maintained
\texttt{lqmm} implementation. Their simulation designs retain at
most 20 predictors, including the requested coordinates, through
a common independent subject-screening step. The double-penalised
comparison uses the same reduced design. Its raw-sum penalty scales
are $\{\tau(1-\tau)N\log p\}^{1/2}$ for fixed coefficients and
$\{\tau(1-\tau)(N/n)\log(nq)\}^{1/2}$ for nuisance coefficients.
The respective multiplier sets are $\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$ and
$\{0.5,1\}$, and the source SIC selects their pair. The
resampling-adjusted random-intercept method uses 100 resamples,
15 quadrature nodes, its source two-step correction and adjusted
standard error. A numerical qualification requires convergence of
the observed fit and every resampled LQMM component. Returned source
calculations and their optimisation messages are retained alongside
that qualification.

Longitudinal SCAD uses the complete HBIC penalty path from
\texttt{geeVerse} version 0.3.1, exchangeable working correlation,
an unpenalised intercept, tolerance $10^{-4}$ and at most 1000
iterations. Matrix products are grouped by cluster size and the
generalised inverse is applied through a LAPACK singular-value
decomposition with the source relative threshold
$1.490116\times10^{-8}$. Each failed penalty has infinite HBIC;
the minimum finite HBIC selects the fit, whose convergence is then
checked. Clinical variables in the application retain their
training-defined units and receive zero penalty factors. Its
screening dimensions are 100, 200 and 400, selected by five inner
subject folds.

The quadratic inference function retains the ordinary inverse
specified in its covariance formula. With $B$ basis matrices and
$p$ fitted coordinates, its empirical moment covariance has rank
at most $n$. Configurations with deficient covariance are reported
as outside that numerical domain. Source-reference checks cover
its moment equations and Newton update, the complete SCAD path,
the mixed-model coefficients and resampling intervals, and the
smoothed quantile correction and variance. These checks also compare
the convex quantile programmes with independent implementations.
